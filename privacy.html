Privacy and Cookie Policy

Interim version 1.0, published 19 November 2026. This policy covers the ShiftNG app and this website, shiftng.org. It is being reviewed by our lawyers and a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation, and may change as a result; any change will be posted here with a new date. Some choices it mentions are still being added to the app, and the policy says so where that applies.

Summary

  • Who we are. ShiftNG is run by ShiftNG. The organisation behind ShiftNG is in the process of registering in Nigeria as a not-for-profit company; its registered name and address will be added here once registration is complete. For any privacy question: privacy@shiftng.org.
  • You can use ShiftNG without an account. Reading budgets, projects, news, elections and courses needs no sign-in.
  • If you make an account, we hold your email address, the fact that you confirmed you are 18 or over, and anything you choose to add or post.
  • We never sell your data, show advertising, use your location for political targeting, or ask for your phone number to sign in.
  • Election volunteers share more, with their explicit consent: a phone number (deleted 30 days after the election) and, if they allow it, where they were when they reported.
  • Our database is in Ireland (European Union). Some providers are in Germany and the United States. Section 8 explains the risks.
  • The usage count can be switched off at any time in Profile. Crash reports carry no names, email addresses, IP addresses or location.
  • You can download your data and delete your account yourself in the app, under Profile. You can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.
  • The website uses no cookies.

1. Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is ShiftNG. The organisation behind ShiftNG is in the process of registering in Nigeria as a not-for-profit company; its registered name and address will be added here once registration is complete ("ShiftNG", "we"). For any privacy question write to privacy@shiftng.org; we are appointing a Data Protection Officer, who will answer at the same address. For app help write to support@shiftng.org; for anything else, contact@shiftng.org.

2. Who can use ShiftNG

Anyone can read ShiftNG without an account. Accounts are for people aged 18 and over. When you sign up we ask your date of birth; it is checked on your phone and never sent to us or stored. We keep only the fact that you confirmed you are 18 or over, and when.

3. What we collect

Without an account

  • The usage count, unless you switch it off (section 6).
  • Crash reports, if the app goes wrong (section 7).
  • Technical details our providers receive when your phone or browser connects, such as your IP address (section 8).

With an account

  • Your email address and password. Your password is held by our sign-in service in a form no one at ShiftNG can read.
  • If you choose: your name (used only to greet you in the app) and where you live (state, local government area and ward). Neither is ever shown publicly.
  • Your Academy courses, quiz attempts and results.
  • What you post: community reports, replies and photos about public projects, "Still the case" taps, reports of content that breaks our rules, and suggested ward councillors. Reports, replies and photos are public, but never show your name or email address; when you add to your own report, it is marked "Update from the reporter". Photos are resized and re-saved on your phone before they are sent, which removes hidden details such as where they were taken.

If you volunteer at an election

  • Your name, email, phone number and polling unit; the wording you accepted and its version; your training status.
  • Your check-ins, result-sheet photos and figures, and incident reports, with the time and, if you allow it, the place on your phone at that moment.
  • Messages between you and the ShiftNG team about one of your reports.

Volunteering may suggest an interest in politics, which the law treats as sensitive. We ask your explicit consent first, never record your party or your vote, and never publish your name or location. See the Volunteer Privacy Notice.

Election team members

If you turn on instant alerts, we keep a code that lets Apple or Google deliver them to your phone.

When you email us

Your email address and what you send.

About people who do not use ShiftNG

ShiftNG also shows information about people who may never use it: public office holders (names, offices, official photos and official contact details, from official and other public sources); people named in news stories we summarise, always with a link to the original; people mentioned in, or visible in photos with, community reports; and election officials and party agents whose names appear on pasted result posters. We show this for public accountability, which is in the public interest and our legitimate interest. If it is about you, you can ask us to correct it, or to remove it where it is not needed for that purpose, at privacy@shiftng.org.

4. Why we use it, and how

  • To run your account, courses and posts, and to show you local information.
  • To publish citizens' reports about public projects and, at elections, checked copies of posted result sheets.
  • To keep ShiftNG safe: to check posts against our Community Guidelines, prevent abuse, and keep a record of changes and of staff access to personal data.
  • To count daily use (unless you switch it off) and to fix crashes.
  • To send emails your account needs, such as confirming your address or resetting your password. We send no newsletters or marketing. If we offer them later, you will have to choose them.

Means of processing. Your data is processed by computer in our database and by our providers' systems listed in section 8. Trained ShiftNG staff and volunteer reviewers see it only as their role requires: for example, a reviewer checks a result sheet against its photo, and a moderator reviews a reported post. We do not make decisions about you by automated means alone, build profiles of your political views, or use your location for political targeting.

6. The usage count

The usage count is on unless you switch it off in Profile, which you can do at any time. In a coming update the app will ask you about it when you first open it. This is exactly what it does:

When this is on, ShiftNG records that this phone opened the app today. It saves a random code on this phone. The code isn't your name, email or account. The app sends that code with the date and whether you were signed in. We use this only to count how many people use ShiftNG each day. The code is replaced every 13 months. Daily records are kept for up to 25 months and then turned into totals only; we are reducing this to six months. It's never shared, sold or used for advertising. Turn this off and nothing is recorded.

7. Crash reports

If the app crashes or goes wrong, it sends a report to our provider Sentry in Germany so we can fix it: what went wrong, the app version and the type of phone. Reports carry no names, email addresses, IP addresses or location, and no screenshots or screen recordings. They are kept for up to 90 days. In a coming update you will be able to switch crash reports off in Profile.

Transfers outside Nigeria. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission has not yet decided that these countries give adequate protection. In a coming update the app will ask your consent, after explaining the risks, when you first open it and when you sign up. The risks are that foreign law, not Nigerian law, governs access to the data by authorities in those countries; that enforcing your rights there may be harder; and that the data is outside Nigeria's direct supervision. Ireland and Germany are covered by the EU's General Data Protection Regulation; the United States has no comparable general law. To reduce the risks, we keep our main database in the EU, send only what each provider needs, encrypt connections and backups, and hold each provider to a data processing agreement. If you do not want your data to go to these providers, you can read ShiftNG's public information on this website without giving any personal data.

We do not otherwise share personal data except where the law requires it, as set out in our Government and Legal Data Requests Policy. We publish how many requests we receive each year.

9. Cookies and similar technologies

Website. shiftng.org uses no cookies, no analytics and no advertising or tracking tools. It has no sign-in, and apart from its search boxes, no forms. The one page that contacts our database is Search all of Nigeria on the Civic page: it sends your search words and filters to our database (Supabase, Ireland), which also receives your IP address and keeps technical logs with it for up to 7 days to run and protect the service. We do not store what you searched for or use these logs to identify anyone. Every other page uses files served from shiftng.org. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages, which receives your IP address when a page loads. News stories, budget documents and election pages link to the websites that published them, which have their own privacy policies.

10. Your rights

You can ask to see the data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, receive it in a portable form, restrict or object to how we use it, and withdraw consent at any time. In the app you can download your data and delete your account yourself (Profile, Your data), and switch the usage count off. You can also ask us to delete your account without the app: see Deleting your account. For anything else write to privacy@shiftng.org. We answer within one month, and sooner when it is urgent. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (ndpc.gov.ng).

12. Keeping your data safe

Access rules are enforced by our database, so each person sees only what their role allows. Staff must give a reason to view anyone's personal details, and every view is recorded. Connections are encrypted, ShiftNG's service accounts use two-step sign-in, and backups are encrypted. If a breach puts your data at risk, we report it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours and tell you without delay if you are likely to be affected.
